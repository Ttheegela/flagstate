# Lobbying around sanctions: who pays, through whom, and what changed

*Trace the Unseen, Sanctions track, 25 September 2026. Two research waves (16 researchers), each report checked by an independent verifier who re-opened the filings and sources. Corrections from the verifiers are applied below.*

**How to read this.** Lobbying is legal. We state what official records show (lobbying disclosures, foreign-agent filings, Treasury notices, votes, court rulings) plainly, with the source. For **effects**, every claim carries a grade:
- **Documented influence:** an official record, ruling or statement ties the outcome to the lobbying or to a named actor's position.
- **Timing fits:** the lobbying came before the change, but no record ties them together.
- **Contested:** other explanations (diplomacy, security reviews, courts, markets, party politics) are at least as strong.

Timing is never treated as proof.

**Sources we used first:** US Lobbying Disclosure Act filings (the Senate's public API, `lda.senate.gov`), US foreign-agent (FARA) filings (`efile.fara.gov`), the Federal Register, US Treasury and Commerce releases, EU law (EUR-Lex) and EU court rulings, congressional votes. Press and think tanks come second and are labelled.

## Part 1. Who lobbies, and through whom

**The pattern: the companies that evade don't lobby; the money moves through respectable names.** None of these has a single US lobbying filing under its own name: RM Design and Development, ELEM Group, Keremet Bank, Zulu Ships Management, Fleet Tanqo, Sovcomflot, Lukoil, Rosneft, Nayara Energy, Litasco, Megasan (checked in the Senate lobbying database). Evasion uses *new* names; lobbying uses *respectable* ones: a parent company, a US subsidiary, a law firm, or a government.

| Case | The clean name on the record | What the record shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Rusal** (US-sanctioned 2018, removed 2019) | Its parent, **En+ Group** | Rusal: 0 lobbying filings. En+ hired **Mercury Public Affairs** under FARA (registration 6170) at **$108,500 a month** from 2019 (cut to $54,250 from January 2020), and **DCI Group** under the lobbying law ($10,000 rising to $40,000 a quarter; issues: low-carbon aluminium, aluminium tariffs), filings through at least early 2025. |
| **Hikvision** (on US Commerce's Entity List) | Its US subsidiary, **Hikvision USA** | Lobbying through **Sidley Austin**, rising from $140,000 (Q2 2018) to $500,000 a quarter (Q2 2019 to Q1 2021), on export controls, foreign relations, homeland security. |
| **SMIC** (Entity List) | A law firm | The filing's client field reads "**Akin Gump on behalf of Semiconductor Manufacturing International Corporation**", with a second firm working under Akin Gump. |
| **Hytera** (Entity List) | A law firm | Lobbying filings through Duane Morris (amounts not re-checked). |
| **Semiconductor chip makers** | Their own names | NVIDIA, AMD, Texas Instruments, Analog Devices, Arrow and the Semiconductor Industry Association lobby openly on export controls. NVIDIA spent **$940,000** in-house in Q1 2025 on "export controls including implementation of P.L. 115-232" (filing ded7018b). The industry association spends $500,000 a quarter in-house, plus a firm filing under "Trade Sanctions". |
| **Getting off a list** | Law firms registered as foreign agents | US law firms register under FARA to seek removal from US sanctions lists for Russia-programme clients (for example registration 7554, 2025; the client's name is blacked out). |
| **Kazakhstan's state oil company, sovereign fund and embassy** | A lobbying firm | Brownstein Hyatt Farber Schreck (FARA 5870): $579,900 for the six months to August 2024. No sanctions wording found in the filings we read. |
| **Lukoil's Bulgarian refinery** | A government | Bulgaria won the EU's only exemption to import Russian crude by sea, for the Lukoil-owned Burgas refinery. Its finance ministry denied lobbying for Lukoil (Politico). |

**Also found:** Russian-linked entities are suspended from the EU Transparency Register (Politico), so they can't lobby EU institutions directly. None of Alrosa, Norilsk Nickel, Belaruskali or Belarusian Potash has a current US, EU or UK register filing.

## Part 2. Did it change anything?

**Main result: no case shows documented causation from a lobbying payment to a sanctions outcome. The strongest documented influence comes from governments protecting their own industries, and it opened exactly the gap the shadow fleet now fills.**

### Shipping and the oil price cap
- In 2022 Greek, Maltese and Cypriot shipowners and their governments turned the EU's proposed ban on shipping Russian oil into the G7 **price cap**, set near the market price.
- In 2025 and 2026, **Greece and Malta are still blocking** a full EU ban on maritime services (documented government positions).
- The shadow fleet became the main channel for Russian oil. Russia's oil revenue was **$13.5 billion in August 2025** (down $0.9 billion).
- **Grade:** documented for the governments' current positions; timing fits for 2022.

### EU carve-outs
- Pipeline oil to Hungary, Slovakia and Czechia (Druzhba), Bulgaria's Burgas exemption, and nickel, palladium and aluminium left off EU lists kept Russian trade flowing for years. These came from **government-to-government negotiation**, not registered lobbying.
- The delay of the EU ban on Russian diamonds (Antwerp, Alrosa): industry lobbying is **reported by the press only**. **Grade: contested.**

### Rusal's removal from the US list
- **6 April 2018**: OFAC listed Oleg Deripaska, En+, Rusal and EuroSibEnergo. **19 December 2018** OFAC told Congress it would remove the companies after a restructuring (the "Barker Plan": an independent chair, reduced voting power for Deripaska).
- **16 January 2019**: Mercury (for En+) filed a paper titled "How the Barker Plan Permanently Removes Oleg Deripaska" with the Justice Department; the **same day** the Senate voted **57 to 42** on blocking the removal (60 needed), so the block failed.
- Deripaska himself **stayed on the US list**, and the EU listed him on 8 April 2022.
- **Grade: timing fits** (downgraded by the verifier from "documented"). Contested: Treasury negotiated the deal, and the vote split largely along party lines.

### AI chip export rules
- Across 2025's back-and-forth (a licence requirement for NVIDIA's H20 in April, the AI Diffusion rule withdrawn on 13 May, H20 licences from August, H200 moved to case-by-case review in January 2026), NVIDIA's in-house lobbying went $940,000 -> $620,000 -> **$1.95 million** -> $1.46 million a quarter, and AMD's ran $1.1 to 1.7 million.
- Outcome: NVIDIA took a **$4.5 billion** H20 write-off and reported only about **$50 million** of H20 revenue after the licences (Q3 fiscal 2026). A charged smuggling scheme (Southern District of New York) sent about **$510 million** of servers to China, part of a $2.5 billion total.
- **Grade: timing fits;** documented influence thin.

### Enforcement capacity
- The **Chip Security Act** (location checks for AI chips, S.1705 / H.R.3447, May 2025) has not moved out of committee while industry lobbying rose.
- The Commerce Department (BIS) is asking Congress for its **largest-ever** enforcement staffing increase (FY2027 request), and US designations fell sharply from their 2024 peak.
- **Grade: timing only;** a policy shift is the other explanation.

### Getting off lists
- The US removes firms from its Russia list routinely with **no visible paid lobbying** (for example Sberbank's Kazakh subsidiary, removed 8 March 2023 after it was sold).
- **EU courts annul** a real share of Russia listings on evidence grounds (**documented** in the rulings).
- Removal rarely means trade resumes: other lists still apply. Megasan came off the main US list on 29 June 2026 (Federal Register 2026-18610) and is still on the Commerce Entity List; no lobbying or foreign-agent filing exists under its name.

### Chinese listed firms
- Licences for US suppliers followed the Commerce Department's licensing policy; **no documented link** to Hikvision's or SMIC's lobbying was found.

### What research says
- Studies show lobbying on sanctions happens and goes with access (for example the "Lobbying EU Sanctions" business survey; Kim and Milner on business and foreign policy). None measures the effect on outcomes causally. Journalism quantifies the money and the revolving door better than the studies quantify impact.

## One line for the demo

"The biggest hole in the oil sanctions wasn't bought by a lobbyist. It was negotiated by EU governments protecting their shipping industries, and the shadow fleet grew into it." And: "Rusal never filed a lobbying disclosure. Its parent did, for $108,500 a month."

## What we could not see

- EU and UK lobbying registers were only lightly searched; Russian-linked entities are barred from the EU register.
- Two figures are unchecked: Sidley Austin's parallel foreign-agent total for Hikvision, and Hytera's amounts.
- Several FARA exhibits black out the client's name.
- Lobbying outside the US disclosure systems (in Brussels, national capitals, or through think tanks and associations) is mostly invisible in public records. The trace of listed companies lobbying through clean intermediaries is in [clean-names-graph/](clean-names-graph/FINDINGS.md): an interactive graph of 63 organisations and 63 links (51 documented).
